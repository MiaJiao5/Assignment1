# Assignment 1 — Why Are We Here?

For designers and artists, code is never merely a tedious technical chore; it is a brush that transforms abstract conceptual ideas into tangible visual reality. Mastery of this tool therefore holds unique significance in contemporary creative practice. While AI can now generate diverse content from natural-language prompts, all its operations remain fundamentally built on code. Without basic programming literacy and with overreliance on AI generation, creators risk losing control over their final work. This scenario is comparable to assigning a physical education teacher to command troops on a battlefield: without knowledge of tactics and military strategy, one cannot lead a team effectively or achieve sustainable long-term success.

I still remember my first encounter with computational parameterization, which resonates with a quote from Dylan Beattie’s 2019 talk The Art of Code: “Code is like a snowflake; every time you run it, it feels like opening a blind box.” Before executing a piece of code, one can hardly predict its final visual output.I recall my first semester at university, when I first experimented with C4D to generate artistic forms through random parameters. Every time I adjusted a parameter, these organically arranged units restructured themselves according to a logic entirely distinct from human cognition, producing an eerie yet captivating beauty. On the one hand, this uncontrollable randomness immediately sparked my curiosity and fascination with computational logic. On the other hand, lacking an understanding of its underlying principles at the time, I regarded these parameters and code as a perilous Pandora’s box: I could never tell whether the results would be desirable, as everything hovered on the verge of chaos. It was precisely this unfamiliar uncertainty that instilled in me a lasting sense of awe and apprehension toward code.

Over the past two years, advancements in AI technology have lowered the barriers to programming to some extent, and I have gradually leveraged AI to create interactive content including games, videos, and webpages. Nevertheless, prominent pain points persist in practical creation. Firstly, while AI can efficiently handle front-end visual presentation, I quickly encounter bottlenecks when dealing with program deployment, underlying hardware adaptation, or network framework construction. AI excels at surface-level visual generation but rarely supports the full-stack technical infrastructure required to complete a finished work. Secondly, when precise modifications are needed, repeated AI iterations through natural language prompts are both time-consuming and error-prone. Subtle, granular adjustments often demand multiple rounds of prompting yet still fail to deliver the intended effects. In contrast, a foundational grasp of programming allows me to identify and resolve such issues far more efficiently. Instead of elaborating my intentions in plain language, I can directly modify the underlying logic of the code to achieve my desired outcomes.

My proficiency improved significantly after completing my first two creative coding practice sessions. I successfully uploaded local application files to a GitHub repository via VS Code and deployed a public website through Cloudflare. Through this process, I gained a new understanding of local-to-cloud deployment workflows and the runtime execution logic of Python code. My previous trepidation about coding gradually faded with consistent hands-on practice and successful deployment experiences. These small accomplishments helped me overcome the fear of system malfunctions and build solid practical confidence. This also demonstrates that a basic mastery of programming languages enables creators to break free from the limitations of generic tools and pursue creation with greater autonomy, grounded in solid technical frameworks and professional knowledge.

Ultimately, learning programming in the AI age is by no means obsolete. It empowers creators to take full control of their creative tools and realize their aesthetic visions and inspirations with higher efficiency. AI acts as a powerful assistant for rapid prototyping, yet it can never replace creators’ ability to define the core logic and creative boundaries of a work. I believe this is the fundamental reason why we should continue learning code in the era of artificial intelligence.
